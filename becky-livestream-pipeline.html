The whole line on one screen

Left to right, then down. The top row happens before VEGAS opens. The shaded region is Vegas Pro 18: the cut lands there as real events, checked, and the finishing passes run on that timeline. Red nodes are steps in Jason's pipeline that becky-livestream does not have yet.

My cut is proven before anything sits on it — and more

Same idea as Jason's proof gate: the VEGAS timeline is compared event-by-event against the plan, then its audio is rebuilt, re-transcribed and lined up with the planned words by timeline time. Mine goes further: it also hunts leftovers and cross-checks two ASR signals before calling a word lost.

His graphics clear a machine check, mine get no QA round

He grades under the graphics, then every graphic passes automated QA plus one fresh-eyes review. My hj-starter FX land AFTER the censor and zoom jobs, and nothing re-checks them — the clearest ordering difference between the two lines.

Where mine is deeper than his

Breaths, wordless stretches, waveform-exact edges, on-camera editor instructions, retake confirmation, censor placement, publish safety, upload copy — all deterministic passes his blueprint simply doesn't contain. His polish is a fixed LUFS target; mine is edge geometry and audio measurement.

Steps · his eight steps, mapped onto my run

What goes in, what comes out, what gets checked — in my pipeline

Each block below keeps Jason's original step description exactly as written, so you can compare one-to-one. Red = I don't do this. Amber = I do it, but wired differently. Green = I do more than he does.

01automatic

Intake his words

Copied, never moved, never touched. The job is named after what the video is about. The format is read from the footage itself; a landscape clip you call a short becomes the Talking Head Explainer, filled to 9:16 once.

In
your raw clip, wherever it landed
Out
a job folder, the format and look picked, a run file any session can resume from, and a list of what must never show when screens are on camera

Mine: the footage is only ever READ (never copied); everything written goes into becky-edit\; fps/duration probed with ffprobe; OBS audio synced by clap first; project named from the folder. I'm missing: the run file mid-job resume concept, and the upfront "what must never show on screen" list (my publish check finds these late, after the edit, instead of at intake).

02AI cut

Rough cut his words

Transcribed once. Filler, dead air, false starts and rambling get cut; say a line twice and the last take wins. Every boundary is measured and the laid cut is proven against the script. Change the cut later and every graphic, sound and caption re-times itself.

Out
the edit decision list and the finished script
Check
every cut measured on the waveform at your timeline's frame rate; on Premiere, the laid dialogue transcribed and matched word for word

Mine (stronger): two-pass transcript (Parakeet + WhisperX second opinion on low confidence), silence map cached, Gemma-4 12B names only exceptions (code confirms every retake against the words), on-camera editor instructions parsed and applied, thinking-islands, viewer-read put-back pass, plan-<model>.json = the EDL. Difference to note: his rule is "last take wins"; mine keeps "the attempt he finishes" and requires the redo to repeat the words. He re-times downstream items on a cut change — I re-run the whole tool (cached, so cheap), which achieves the same determinism.

03measured

Audio polish

Measured every time, never a fixed number. Mono lands exactly like stereo. On the explainer, a second voice through a phone or speaker is lifted when it sits more than 3 dB under.

Target
kept speech at −17 LUFS into a −6 dBFS limiter
How
measured on every video; live on Premiere, baked on Resolve, CapCut and Final Cut

This is the single biggest result gap. becky-livestream never touches speech loudness: no LUFS measurement, no limiter, no gain normalization anywhere in the run (searched: zero hits). Every cut changes perceived level; his draft arrives broadcast-consistent, mine arrives raw-mic. Build a becky-audiopolish VEGAS pass: measure kept speech LUFS per piece, apply bus/event gain into a limiter, verify by measuring the rendered timeline.

04camera only

Grade his words

Before the graphics and underneath them, so the graphic colours never shift. Long-form and the Talking Head Explainer only. Screen-recording footage stays bare. No correction step.

Look
Autumn-Rec709 at 80 intensity, 120 saturation
How
adjustment layer on Premiere, colour group on Resolve, one baked LUT on Final Cut

Mine: I DO grade — hj-starter track FX (Beauty Box, AutoLooks, Color Corrector, Color Curves ×2) on the main video track. But the order differs: videovegas.go applies them AFTER the edit check, censor, and zoom jobs — i.e. ON TOP of later timeline changes, and the B-roll/assets inserts ride above that track. His invariant: grade goes down before graphics and underneath them so graphic colors never shift. Move the starter-FX job ahead of the asset/censor/zoom jobs in the sequence.

05graphics

Graphics and captions his words

Long-form gets text animations, push-ins and full-screen scenes on a set cadence. The explainer rotates layouts on hard cuts (split, tight, wide, full, card), planned per sentence. Screen captures are planned, a still of you is picked from your best frames, and on-screen secrets are found and blurred.

Stages
storyboard, build, review
Check
an automated QA pass, one fresh-eyes review round, the fixes re-checked by the QA pass

Mine: slow creep 1.11× / still crops ~once a minute ≈ his push-ins on a cadence ✓; assets/B-roll + MARKERS.txt ✓; secrets are FOUND (publish check OCR+vision) but only marked as regions — never BLURRED ✗; no long-form burned-in captions (his captions are a graphic built from the finished script; mine exist only in the vertical shorts tool) ✗; no hero-frame still picker for long-form ✗; and crucially NO storyboard→build→review stage machine and no automated graphics-QA gate — placement is fire-and-forget BeckyFX jobs.

06automatic

Sound effects

Planned from the graphics as they sit on the timeline. The explainer has its own map: the literal object on a visible event, face and captions silent. Repeated sounds stay on one layer; stacked cues are held under −1 dBTP.

In
the placed graphics
How
planned from every motion event, placed in your app and read back

Mine: the only SFX in my pipeline is the censor hit (with its own audio-edge finder — genuinely better than a blind placement). No whooshes on zooms/crops, no cue under inserted assets, no peak-limiting of stacked cues. A deterministic version fits my philosophy perfectly: every BeckyFX zoom/crop/insert event already knows its time and kind → a table maps kind→sound→level, a dedicated SFX track places them, then read back.

+opt-in

Music bed only when asked

A flat bed under the voice, after SFX and before review. Ducking and fade-in are opt-in on top.

Level
−20 dB from the song's sustained entrance, 2 s tail
When
after SFX, before review

Mine: absent from the YouTube workflow (opt-in flag, locked level numbers, its own track — trivially deterministic once audio polish exists to keep the bed honest under −17 LUFS speech).

07you

Review his words

Taste only: the draft already has zero objective defects. Changes re-render just the part that changed. Anything you change by hand in the app stays changed.

In
a draft with zero objective defects
Out
picture lock

Mine: human review on the timeline IS the endpoint — good. But his precondition "zero objective defects" is enforced by 30+ checks across steps 2–6; mine is enforced by the edit check + publish check only (and see the red steps above: un-leveled audio is an objective defect waiting for me). Hand-edit protection: my rerun won't overwrite a project you saved yourself ✓ (same promise as his "stays yours").

08in your app

Export

Premiere exports when you say go, and a built-in encoder steps in wherever an app's own can't hit the numbers. One command then names the final and drops a copy in Downloads. An ad cut or a 4K Shorts file is one flag.

Long
3840×2160, CBR 50 (80 at 48 to 60 fps), even from a 1080 timeline
Vertical
1080×1920 CBR 14 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, plus 2160×3840 for Shorts from 4K
Audio
AAC 320k, bt709

Mine: the long-form run STOPS at the reviewed project — no locked export spec, no render template for the YouTube master (the vertical shorts tool does render via the Voukoder "TikTok NVENC" template, so the plumbing exists). Add a locked-spec render step + one post-review command.

Going further · the honest math, my version

His listed problems — solved, partially solved, or still open for me

✓solved

Cuts that land early his trap #1

Premiere's scripting rounds every cut twice, so on mixed-frame-rate footage each cut landed about 41 ms early. You only find it by measuring the waveform.

Mine
SOLVED and then some: tighten() moves every edge onto the first/last frame of voice using mic-relative levels; verify() re-transcribes the actual timeline output rather than trusting timestamps; Parakeet's early-first-word drift is explicitly compensated.
✓solved

The wrong font, silently his trap #4

The graphics renderer swaps Helvetica for a lookalike without a warning. Nobody notices until the side-by-side.

Mine
N/A→solved by architecture: VEGAS uses the installed FX/caption engines; nothing renders text off-app that could silently substitute. (If I add long-form burned captions, this trap becomes live — budget for a QA check.)
~partial

Audio that pumps and clicks his trap #3

The obvious loudness fix breathes on every pause. Per-clip effects click at every cut.

Mine
PARTIALLY avoided: my base is silencedetect removal with edges snapped to voice frames, so most pause-pumping never appears. But because I do NO loudness normalization (step 03), the pumping risk hasn't been met yet — when I add LUFS leveling, this exact trap will arrive. Do it per-piece on the timeline with measured makeup, not a whole-project compressor.
✗open

A timeline call that deletes your timeline his trap #2

One Resolve scripting call wipes whole clips off the timeline while reporting success.

Mine
OPEN CLASS OF BUG, partly mitigated: verify() would catch mass deletion after the fact, and projects are never overwritten. Not mitigated: a pre/post sanity snapshot around each BeckyFX job (event counts + total duration before/after, refuse-save on mismatch). Cheap to add, matches his "checks their own work" doctrine.
✗open

The problem his whole page hides for both of us

Starts from a diagram; the traps cost hours each; three months of daily building.

Mine
I have ALREADY paid the cutting half of this tax (that's why the result "listens" good). The remaining tax is the finishing half — steps 3–6 and 8 — and those are mostly deterministic measurement + placement problems, exactly the kind becky-tools was built for. Estimated order of payoff: audio polish > export specs > graphics QA/order > SFX > music bed.

His starter prompt vs my system — the structural difference

His prompt makes Claude Code BUILD the pipeline from scratch on a blank machine, then OPERATE it live through MCP, with one human gate: approve cuts.json before anything is laid. Mine skips the agent-at-runtime entirely: the pipeline is pre-built Go + C# scripts, the model only answers "what to keep", and the only human gate is the final timeline review.

What his prompt has that mine-equivalent lacks

(1) the explicit cut-list approval step before building, (2) environment self-check + "prove the connection inside that project only" handshake, (3) honesty report listing what the run did NOT do, (4) "write non-obvious learnings into CLAUDE.md" memory loop, (5) "if something fails twice, stop and tell me". Items 1, 3 and 5 are one-day adds to becky-livestream and directly attack the frustration cycle.

Where my prompt philosophy beats his

He lets the LLM choose cut times with fixed pads (0.08/0.12 s) and admits words still clip — I derive edges from the waveform. He trusts one WhisperX pass — I use Parakeet + WhisperX second opinion. He asks a giant model to freehand the edit — I ask a small model only for exceptions and confirm them in code. His own closing line concedes it: "anything that CAN be deterministic SHOULD be" is his end-state, not his start-state. Mine starts there.
